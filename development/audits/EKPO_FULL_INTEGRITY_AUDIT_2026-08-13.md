The principal unresolved integrity layer is **cultural language register**.

The local Calabar characters are psychologically distinct, but portions of their English remain too uniformly screenplay-polished and North-American-neutral. Before the Sequences 1-7 elevation candidate is promoted and before the screenplay is considered production-grade dialogue, Bode, Eno, Eyo and other local household speakers require a deliberate Nigerian English / Nigerian Pidgin / Efik register pass.

This does not mean `add more Pidgin` indiscriminately.

It means code-switching must become part of character action.

## Nigerian language/register integrity

### FAIL UNTIL INTEGRATED

The screenplay currently demonstrates one strong code-switch model at the airport:

- porter speaks Pidgin;
- Bode answers in Pidgin/Nigerian local register;
- Bode then returns to clearer English with his Canadian-raised nephews.

This logic needs to extend through the compound.

Required system:

- **Malik/Ade/Kai:** Canadian English; limited inherited/local vocabulary according to actual familiarity.
- **Bode:** Nigerian English by default with nephews; Pidgin in peer/local/urgent/informal exchanges; Efik in verified kinship/intimacy contexts.
- **Eno:** Nigerian English with nephews; Pidgin for selected household correction, humor, urgency and local-family interaction; Efik for verified greeting/intimacy contexts.
- **Eyo/local adults:** freer local code-switching, distinct from Bode's age/temperament.

Do not write accent phonetically.

Do not treat Nigerian Pidgin as damaged Standard English.

Do not over-translate lines whose social meaning is clear from action.

Final production dialogue requires Nigerian/Calabar/Efik language review.

See `docs/CULTURAL_LANGUAGE_REGISTER.md`.
