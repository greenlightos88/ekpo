## Verdict

The integrated screenplay through Sequence 12 passes EKPO's current **story, canon, character, horror, physiology, escalation, exposition, render, and structural execution gates**.

A new integrity pass identified one unresolved production-grade layer: **Calabar cultural language register**.

Bode, Eno, Eyo and local household speakers require a controlled Nigerian English / Nigerian Pidgin / Efik register pass so their speech reflects actual local life, audience, status, intimacy and pressure rather than defaulting too often to neutral North-American screenplay cadence.

This is not a story failure and does not invalidate the accepted horror/character architecture. It does mean the project should **not be called final production-ready dialogue** until the register pass is integrated and reviewed.

This quality gate is not a prediction or guarantee of awards. It means the current pages have earned continuation under the project's standards while one authenticity layer remains open.

## Sequence 12 — `WHEN IT STAYS`

**HORROR / CHARACTER / PHYSIOLOGY PASS. CULTURAL EMERGENCY-REGISTER PASS REQUIRED.**

The sequence cashes the film's accumulated restraint without turning the Entity into a conventional attacking monster.

The accepted horror engine remains perceptual/physiological incompatibility:

- metallic taste and pressure;
- delayed/misaligned sound;
- binocular convergence failure;
- violent vertigo/proprioceptive fracture;
- perceptual stutter;
- discontinuity between action and experienced movement;
- breathing-sequence disruption;
- ocular/nasal bleeding;
- salivation/vomiting;
- transient motor/sensory failure;
- apparent structural load;
- residual injury after withdrawal.

The defining visual inversion remains that the Entity becomes more perceptually coherent while human bodies and local space become less coherent.

The unresolved language task is specific: when Eno/Eyo/local relatives move the household under real danger, their speech must not become generic North-American emergency dialogue. Register should shift naturally according to recipient and urgency.

## Nigerian language/register gate

**OPEN / REQUIRED BEFORE PRODUCTION-GRADE LOCK.**

Nigerian Pidgin must not be treated as broken English or decorative slang.

Code-switching must serve relationship and social action.

Active register control:

- `docs/CULTURAL_LANGUAGE_REGISTER.md`
- `development/rewrites/CULTURAL_LANGUAGE_REGISTER_PASS.md`

Required principles:

- Malik/Ade/Kai retain Canadian-raised language patterns.
- Bode primarily uses Nigerian English with the nephews, with Pidgin/Efik shifts according to local audience, intimacy, urgency or solidarity.
- Eno primarily uses Nigerian English with the nephews, with selected Pidgin/Efik shifts for household command, teasing, correction, urgency and kinship.
- Eyo/local adults may code-switch more freely without becoming duplicates of Bode/Eno.
- no phonetic accent writing;
- no random Pidgin seasoning;
- plot-critical meaning remains clear;
- final extended Pidgin/Efik wording requires Nigerian/Calabar/Efik review.
