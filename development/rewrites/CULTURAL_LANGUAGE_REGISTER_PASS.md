# EKPO — CULTURAL LANGUAGE REGISTER PASS

Status: CANDIDATE / NOT AUTHORITATIVE
Date: 2026-08-13
Applies to: elevated opening candidate + authoritative Sequences 8-12

This is a **register pass**, not a `slang pass`.

All Pidgin/Efik wording below is provisional until qualified Nigerian/Calabar/Efik review. The dramatic placement of code-switching is the primary proposal.

## Governing rule

Preserve clear English when a local character is deliberately accommodating Malik/Ade/Kai.

Switch toward Nigerian Pidgin/Efik/Nigerian-English cadence when social pressure makes that register more natural:

- local-to-local interaction;
- teasing;
- household command;
- disbelief;
- irritation;
- urgency;
- intimate kinship/grief.

Do not translate every switch.

## Bode register throughout Sequences 8-12

Do not substantially Pidginize Bode's mythology-adjacent dialogue.

He is deliberately trying to be understood by three Canadian-raised nephews while speaking about something he does not fully understand himself.

Instead:

- trim polished constructions;
- use shorter Nigerian-English syntax;
- allow Pidgin with Eno/Eyo/local adults in private or urgent side exchanges;
- allow an Efik return under grief/intimacy only after cultural review.

Bode's localness should be demonstrated by **switching appropriately**, not by keeping him permanently in one code.

## Integrity gate before promotion

After applying any language changes:

1. Cover names: Bode/Eno/Eyo must remain distinguishable from one another.
2. Remove Pidgin words mentally: syntax/social behavior should still feel locally rooted.
3. Confirm Malik/Ade/Kai have not acquired false local fluency.
4. Confirm code-switching has a social reason.
5. Confirm plot-critical information remains comprehensible.
6. Obtain Nigerian/Calabar/Efik review before final production lock.

The standard is not `sounds Nigerian to a North American ear`.

The standard is `sounds like this particular person speaking to this particular person in this particular house.`
