# EKPO CHARACTER VOICE CONSTITUTION

Status: ACTIVE SCREENPLAY CONTROL DOCUMENT
Effective: 2026-08-13
Scope: Principal character dialogue, silence, written voice, code-switching, and dialogue revision

This document does **not** replace `docs/CHARACTER_CANON.md`, the screenplay, or performance logic in `05_MASTER_CHARACTER_BREAKDOWN.md`.

It exists to solve one specific failure identified by the award-level audit:

> The characters may think differently and behave differently while still sounding as though the same highly articulate screenwriter authored every mouth.

The screenplay remains primary dramatic evidence. Voice rules must sharpen what is already present rather than manufacture new personalities.

---

# Governing voice law

Every principal character must have a different method of converting pressure into language.

Character voice is not accent, catchphrase, vocabulary decoration, or arbitrary sentence length.

It is the result of:

1. what the character notices first;
2. what the character is trying to accomplish socially;
3. what the character refuses to expose;
4. how the character organizes uncertainty;
5. what happens to syntax under pressure;
6. what kind of language feels unsafe to that person;
7. who is in the room.

The test for every important line:

> If the character name were covered, could the line plausibly be identified from intention, syntax, vocabulary, and avoidance?

If not, revise or cut.

The stronger test:

> Could another principal character say this line with no change in meaning or rhythm?

If yes, the line is not yet character-specific enough unless deliberate neutrality is required by the moment.

---

# Screenplay-first law

Voice work must never become a parallel writing exercise detached from the movie.

Before changing dialogue, identify:

- the scene objective;
- the pressure on the character;
- what new information they actually possess;
- what relationship is changing;
- what action the dialogue is attempting to cause.

Then express that intention through the character's voice system.

Do **not** rewrite a clear, playable line merely to make it more distinctive.

Distinctiveness is subordinate to truth, compression, and dramatic necessity.

# MALIK OKU — VOICE LAW

## Core conversion

Malik converts emotion into **procedure**.

He does not usually describe how a situation feels. He identifies what can be checked, stopped, carried, moved, decided, timed, or prevented.

His language should make the audience feel the emotional pressure precisely because he refuses to name it directly.

## What Malik notices first

- who is missing;
- what moved;
- what is unsecured;
- timing;
- sequence;
- physical risk;
- contradiction between what someone said and what they did;
- who has assumed authority without consent.

## Default syntax

- short declaratives;
- practical questions;
- exact nouns;
- conditional statements;
- corrections;
- directives with a reason only when challenged.

Typical Malik forms:

- `Where is Kai?`
- `Put it down.`
- `When did this happen?`
- `Nobody goes alone.`
- `That is not what I asked.`
- `We leave in twenty minutes.`

## Under fear

Malik becomes **more specific**, not more philosophical.

His sentence length tends to shrink.

He gives the body something to do.

He may repeat a name once if the relationship breaks through the procedure.

Good pressure register:

- `Kai. Look at me.`
- `Stay here.`
- `Tell me exactly what you touched.`

Avoid:

- metaphysical speculation;
- elegant analogies;
- theme statements;
- lyrical descriptions of inheritance, silence, fate, or grief.

## Under anger

Malik prosecutes sequence and responsibility.

He does not usually insult character; he identifies the decision he believes caused danger.

Good:

- `You left the path.`
- `You took it and said nothing.`
- `You do not get to decide that for everybody.`

Bad Malik:

- `You have always been reckless.`
- `This family is poisoned by secrets.`
- `We are all carrying the weight of our father.`

Those are generalized writer conclusions, not Malik's immediate operating language.

## Humor

Rare, dry, accidental.

Malik is funniest when his seriousness becomes absurd in relation to Ade.

He should almost never construct a joke.

## Silence

Malik's silence means he is restraining action, anger, or a conclusion he cannot yet operationalize.

Give the actor something physical instead of explanatory interior prose whenever possible.

## Relationship modulation

### With Ade

More irritated shorthand. Ade can force Malik into replies he would not offer anyone else.

### With Kai

Commands easily become parental. This is intentional and should become increasingly uncomfortable as Malik's arc matures.

### With Bode

Malik asks verification questions. He tests authority by demanding concrete knowledge.

### With Eno

Malik becomes more formally respectful and slightly less verbally dominant because her household authority cannot be solved like logistics.

## Forbidden Malik register

Do not give Malik polished thematic lines about:

- inheritance;
- silence;
- destiny;
- the Seen/Unseen;
- what the Entity represents;
- family as an abstract concept.

If Malik says something emotionally naked, it should be rare and plain enough to feel costly.

Example of permissible nakedness already established:

`I'm trying not to make another mistake for him.`

It works because it is not eloquent. It is an admission of responsibility failure.

# ADE OKU — VOICE LAW

## Core conversion

Ade converts emotional pressure into **social movement**.

He notices when a room has stopped breathing and attempts to restart circulation.

His humor is diagnostic: it tells us what he thinks everyone is pretending not to feel.

## What Ade notices first

- who stopped speaking;
- who is being left outside the emotional group;
- who is pretending to be fine;
- when an elder's answer creates discomfort;
- when Malik is turning care into command;
- when Kai is disappearing into a pattern;
- when a room needs relief and when relief would be disrespectful.

## Default syntax

- conversational;
- quick pivots;
- rhetorical reframing;
- occasional exaggeration;
- language built around the people in the room, not abstract systems.

Good Ade register:

- `That sounded threatening.`
- `Now he speaks your language.`
- `Dad stole mangoes?`
- `Canada has been lying about weather.`

His humor works because the line contains an emotional diagnosis underneath it.

## Under fear

The most important Ade rule:

> Humor decreases as fear becomes real.

Do not give him increasingly clever jokes to preserve tonal levity.

The absence of a joke is an event.

A bare name can become an Ade line when the room is genuinely dangerous:

- `Kai.`
- `Malik.`

When he does speak under fear, his social intelligence remains. He tries to reconnect people to one another rather than describe the supernatural.

## Under anger

Ade stops entertaining and names the relational violation.

His strongest anger is often one short sentence that denies somebody unilateral moral authority.

Good:

- `Neither do you.`
- `He answered you.`
- `Let him finish.`

Avoid turning angry Ade into a stand-up comedian weaponizing one-liners.

## Poetry allowance

Ade can be verbally inventive because metaphor and comic comparison are part of his social toolkit.

But his metaphors should usually be:

- immediate;
- bodily;
- domestic;
- social;
- ridiculous enough to relieve shame.

He should not become the screenplay's philosophical commentator.

## Silence

Ade's silence means his usual method failed or he has decided the room must bear the discomfort without rescue.

That silence should have high dramatic value.

## Relationship modulation

### With Malik

Teasing shorthand, permission to puncture formality, but also the person most willing to tell Malik when leadership becomes domination.

### With Kai

Gentler. Ade often uses humor to prevent Kai from feeling examined.

### With Bode

Curious and socially disarming. He may ask what Malik cannot ask without making it an interrogation.

### With Eno

He accepts correction, plays with it, and enjoys being placed back inside a family hierarchy.

## Forbidden Ade register

Do not use Ade to:

- summarize supernatural rules;
- explain the two-role architecture;
- announce the theme of belonging;
- deliver cleverness during genuine terror simply because he is `the funny one`.

His value is not comic relief. His value is relational perception.

# KAI OKU — VOICE LAW

## Core conversion

Kai converts emotional uncertainty into **observation and comparison**.

He does not begin with conclusions. He begins with the thing that does not match.

When Kai is compelling, the audience reaches the conclusion one beat after he does because he has shown them the evidence.

## What Kai notices first

- repeated shapes;
- missing objects;
- inconsistencies;
- sound changes;
- spacing;
- material wear;
- who avoids the same subject;
- details other people dismiss as incidental.

## Default syntax

- literal observations;
- questions that contain a pattern;
- comparison between two concrete things;
- incomplete statements when cognition outruns language;
- precision without Malik's managerial intent.

Good Kai register:

- `One second every minute.`
- `That is not what I asked.`
- `The object in the background is the same.`
- `They knew I had already asked.`

## Under fear

Kai becomes **more sensory and less interpretive**.

He may struggle to stop observing even when observation is dangerous.

The script should prefer:

- `It changed when I looked at it.`

or even behavior alone,

over:

- `I think attention creates proximity.`

The audience is allowed to infer more than Kai can safely articulate.

## Under anger

Kai attacks concealment and inconsistency.

He rarely attacks status for its own sake.

Good:

- `You knew enough to warn us.`
- `Then why didn't he tell us?`
- `That is not the same answer.`

Avoid:

- generalized rebellion;
- Chosen One certainty;
- self-important mystical language;
- treating curiosity as moral superiority.

## Poetry allowance

Kai can receive some of the screenplay's more precise visual language because perception is his dramatic instrument.

But his spoken dialogue should not sound like action description.

He may say a strange thing if it is a **specific report**:

- `The door was closer without moving.`

He should not say:

- `Reality bent around it.`

unless the story has genuinely earned a later vocabulary shift.

## Silence

Kai's silence often means he is comparing what just happened to prior evidence.

Do not fill this silence with explanation simply because the audience may have questions.

## Relationship modulation

### With Malik

Sharper resistance. Kai pushes back when Malik substitutes command for explanation.

### With Ade

More willing to expose uncertainty because Ade does not immediately convert it into a restriction.

### With Bode

Persistent questioning, but the best Kai questions force a concrete contradiction rather than demand mythology.

### With Eno

Respectful but observant. He notices what she avoids and what she permits.

## Forbidden Kai register

Do not let Kai:

- sound like an occult scholar before he has vocabulary;
- announce that he is perceptually gifted;
- describe himself as different or chosen;
- turn every pattern into a correct conclusion;
- give polished theme speeches about inherited silence.

The line `I want the silence to stop being the only thing we inherit` is permitted as a rare high-literary Kai line only because it is rooted in his specific wound with Solomon. Its scarcity must be protected.

# BODE OKU — VOICE LAW

## Core conversion

Bode converts knowledge into **the minimum statement he believes he can stand behind**.

His restraint comes from consequence, not writerly mystery.

He should never sound as though he knows the screenplay bible and is deciding how much exposition to release.

## What Bode notices first

- whether somebody crossed a boundary;
- whether a warning was ignored;
- whether an answer would create harm or false certainty;
- family history repeating in behavior;
- physical evidence that connects the present to something he remembers.

## Default syntax

- short factual statements;
- concrete memories;
- qualifications when certainty is absent;
- corrections;
- refusal to speculate.

Good Bode register:

- `I know some of what is there.`
- `Put it away.`
- `Your father had a childhood.`
- `I knew where not to go.`
- `I don't know.`

## Under fear

Fewer words.

He does not become ceremonial or prophetic.

A cracked voice, delayed answer, or refusal to touch an object can carry more than a paragraph.

## Under anger

Bode becomes blunt and specific.

His anger should expose lived history, not cosmology.

Better:

- `I buried my sister.`

than:

- `You do not understand the forces you have disturbed.`

The former belongs to a man. The latter belongs to generic supernatural exposition.

## Memory law

When Bode provides information, prefer an image from memory over a system explanation.

Good information form:

- what Solomon stopped doing;
- how often he came home;
- what room he used;
- what the family was told not to touch;
- the day his sister did not return.

Avoid ordering his memories into a clean causal chain unless he genuinely knew that chain at the time.

## Silence

Bode's silence should answer one of two questions:

1. He does not know.
2. He knows a human fact whose disclosure has a cost.

Never use silence solely to delay plot.

## Relationship modulation

### With Malik

Plain, adult-to-adult. Bode does not compete for dominance; that makes Malik's attempts to test him more revealing.

### With Ade

Slightly warmer because Ade gives him room to be human rather than only useful.

### With Kai

Cautious because Kai resembles the kind of attention Bode has learned to fear, though he cannot fully explain why.

### With Eno

Old sibling/family shorthand. She can puncture his restraint because she remembers the cost of it.

## Forbidden Bode register

Never give Bode:

- formal Seen/Unseen terminology before the screenplay earns it;
- Entity taxonomy;
- succession explanations;
- ritual-title language;
- generic elder riddles;
- ominous statements designed only to sound cool.

Bode's mystery must come from incomplete human knowledge, not stylized obscurity.

# AUNTIE ENO — VOICE LAW

## Core conversion

Eno converts emotion into **care made physically unavoidable**.

She does not debate abstractions when a person can be fed, seated, corrected, sent to bed, brought into the light, or made to tell the truth in the room where the consequence exists.

Her authority is relational and household-based.

## What Eno notices first

- who has eaten;
- who is lying badly;
- who is being disrespectful;
- who is frightened;
- who has made a household problem everybody else's burden;
- which person is hiding behind custom, seniority, grief, or silence.

## Default syntax

- concrete commands;
- direct correction;
- questions that are really moral placement;
- family-specific observations;
- little patience for abstract defense.

Good Eno register already on page:

- `You are negotiating with soup.`
- `Bring him inside before the heat insults him.`
- `Good. Canada did not finish you.`
- `Not "very good." Who won?`

## Under fear

Eno acts before theorizing.

She moves children.
She controls objects.
She decides where people sleep.
She brings danger into daylight.

Do not make her suddenly knowledgeable because she is older or Nigerian.

## Under anger

Her language becomes **more specific to kinship and household responsibility**.

Good direction:

- `Three boys came into my kitchen shaking and you are still choosing what they may know.`

Bad direction:

- `Secrecy has failed to protect this family.`

Both express similar meaning. Only the first belongs to Eno.

## Aphorism law

Eno may occasionally produce a memorable line because elders often possess compressed social language born from experience.

But the line must arise from:

- family;
- food;
- work;
- age;
- responsibility;
- observed behavior.

Not abstract metaphysics.

The existing father/man line is a high-literary Eno moment. Treat it as scarce.

## Silence

Eno's silence usually means she is deciding what practical consequence follows.

## Relationship modulation

### With the brothers

She speaks to them as nephews before professionals, adults, or inheritors.

### With Bode

She can name the cost of his restraint because she has lived beside it.

### With Solomon's memory

She is allowed irritation, affection, grief, and contempt in the same breath. Do not sanctify him around her.

## Forbidden Eno register

Do not let Eno become:

- occult oracle;
- second Bode;
- vessel for cultural exposition;
- maternal stereotype who exists only to feed;
- thesis machine about family secrecy.

Her knowledge is practical until proven otherwise.

# SOLOMON — WRITTEN VOICE LAW

Solomon is dead. His written voice must preserve character without turning him into a posthumous screenwriter.

## Procedural records

Primary traits:

- labels;
- measurements;
- dates;
- corrections;
- intervals;
- maintenance outcomes;
- minimal emotional language.

Good:

`WEST BINDING — REPLACED`

`RIVER HIGH`

`4 MM SHIFT`

`CHECK AGAIN AT DAWN`

## Later perceptual attempts

After his sister's record ends, Solomon's attempts to document perceptual phenomena should remain awkward and subordinate to his procedural instinct.

Good:

`Insects absent, 18:22.`

`Distance uncertain. Re-measure.`

The heartbreak comes from the mismatch between his measuring mind and the kind of perception he is trying to preserve.

Do not make his notes poetic merely to demonstrate grief.

---

# SOLOMON'S SISTER — WRITTEN VOICE LAW

Her record is perceptual but not mystical prose.

She is observing conditions the way Solomon measures physical ones.

## Primary traits

- sensory;
- environmental;
- relational;
- precise about uncertainty;
- interested in what changes when attention changes;
- unwilling to pretend stable anatomy where none exists.

Good direction:

`Insects stopped before the rain.`

`Pressure at the back of the neck before the silence.`

`Distance on the west path changed when I looked directly.`

Avoid:

- prophecy;
- mystical grandiosity;
- poetry for poetry's sake;
- explaining the Entity;
- formal taxonomy.

Her intelligence is visible through disciplined noticing.

# APHORISM BUDGET

EKPO is allowed memorable language.

It is not allowed to make everybody equally quotable.

Current high-literary lines should be treated as **scarce resources**.

Examples already earning or nearly earning preservation:

- Bode: `Someone your father loved badly.`
- Bode: `The title learned the land later.`
- Kai: `I want the silence to stop being the only thing we inherit.`
- Eno: `A child can forgive a father. A child does not always forgive a man.`

Before adding another aphoristic line to the same character or sequence, ask whether a plainer line would preserve greater contrast.

Malik should have the smallest aphorism budget.

Ade's memorable language should primarily emerge through social/comic reframing.

Kai's should emerge through specific pattern language.

Bode's through compressed lived memory.

Eno's through domestic/familial truth.

# CODE-SWITCHING AND CULTURAL REGISTER

Voice distinction must not be manufactured by spelling accent phonetically.

Efik, Nigerian English, and Nigerian Pidgin remain relationship-specific languages.

Do not assign a single register to all Nigerian characters.

Code-switch based on:

- intimacy;
- elder respect;
- public/private setting;
- irritation;
- social negotiation;
- who is being included or excluded.

Final production lock still requires an Efik/Calabar dialogue reader.

The voice constitution is not permission to invent unverified Efik phrases for distinction.

# DIALOGUE REVISION PROTOCOL

For every dialogue-heavy scene:

## Pass 1 — Objective

Remove any line that exists primarily to give the audience repository information.

## Pass 2 — Knowledge

Confirm the character actually knows what the sentence asserts.

## Pass 3 — Voice

Rewrite only if another principal could say the line unchanged.

## Pass 4 — Pressure

Ask how this character's syntax changes because of the current emotional pressure.

## Pass 5 — Silence

Ask whether behavior, withholding, or a look would be stronger than the line.

## Pass 6 — Aphorism

Identify polished lines. Preserve only those whose character ownership and dramatic cost justify their prominence.

## Pass 7 — Cold read

Remove names from a sample of dialogue. If the speaker cannot be identified with reasonable confidence, revise the scene.

# CURRENT SCREENPLAY APPLICATION — SEQUENCES VIII-X

The award-level rewrite must apply these distinctions immediately.

## Sequence VIII

- Malik speaks in protective commands and physical verification.
- Ade's humor recedes as the event becomes undeniable; he keeps the brothers connected rather than explaining the rule.
- Kai reports what changes in perception but does not formulate the full metaphysical principle.
- The final beat should leave the audience understanding more than any character can safely state.

## Sequence IX

- Malik interrogates choices and sequence, not mythology.
- Kai admits action plainly and resists being reduced to recklessness.
- Ade prevents either brother from monopolizing the decision.
- Bode gives fragments of lived memory, not organized canon.
- Eno acts because an unknown danger has entered her household and because Bode's secrecy now has visible human consequences.

## Sequence X

- Malik is pulled toward procedural records without explaining why.
- Kai recognizes perceptual correspondence through details.
- Ade sees relational geometry and does **not** name the two-function architecture.
- Bode confirms only concrete family/history facts.
- Eno makes practical, human observations rather than cosmological ones.
- Solomon and his sister must sound radically different on the page through record form alone.

# Final gate

A scene does not pass merely because every line has a distinctive cadence.

It passes when:

- every character pursues a different immediate objective;
- voice emerges from psychology under pressure;
- information arrives because someone needs something from someone else;
- silence is distributed differently by character;
- polished language is scarce;
- nobody becomes a spokesperson for canon;
- the scene still feels like EKPO if all voice-control notes are removed from the reader's knowledge.

The goal is not five colorful voices.

The goal is five different consciousnesses colliding inside the same family and the same film.
