# EKPO — Pressure-Chamber Production and Safety Plan

Status: DEVELOPMENT DESIGN / PROFESSIONAL APPROVAL REQUIRED
Updated: 2026-08-24
Scope: Sequences 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 and 23–26

## Governing dramatic hierarchy

The chamber is an ancient working boundary interface, not a torture device, sci-fi portal, handyman workshop or monster cage. For every major beat, the audience must read information in this order:

1. **Water/reflection:** which pressure is entering and whether a rounded response follows.
2. **Red anchored lines:** where the load is traveling.
3. **Human bodies:** who is carrying it, where the exit is blocked and what the injuries cost.
4. **Threshold/Entity:** a restrained, dangerous background correction, never a second action spectacle.

One dominant new piece of visual information per shot. Do not ask viewers to read water, lines, three faces and the inner door simultaneously.

## Practical set geography

- One reproducible ancient dome, approximately 14–18 feet across with a 12–16 foot apex, reached by a deep turning stone staircase.
- Maintained inner door centered opposite the stair; practical work area along the near curved wall; central stained working circle; WEST/shrine guide on one side; river-return guide and practical stop on the other.
- All visible red lines terminate at identifiable fixed guides. Brass and copper indicate hardware, not supernatural recognition.
- Behind the narrowly opening inner door, build only a shallow practical stone antechamber. Forced perspective, controlled light and carefully limited negative space may suggest incompatible depth. No performer enters an impossible-space effect.
- Keep stair egress, crew lanes, fire exits and emergency access physically unobstructed even when camera framing suggests a closed environment.
- Photograph and diagram baseline line positions, band rest marks, index angle, water level, floor stain and prop custody before every setup.

## Bands, anchored lines and simulated load

- Design brass forearm bands as lightweight breakaway or remotely releasable costume facades; all apparent closures must open immediately on performer cue.
- Use slack management, edited inserts, concealed practical actuation and camera angle to imply directional tension. Never use an actor's actual neck, chest, joints, shoulder, ankles or breathing as the load-bearing point.
- Any necessary support harness, concealed rig, stunt double or mechanical cue must be designed, fitted, operated and approved by qualified stunt and rigging personnel for the actual set and performer.
- No line may form an inescapable loop. Provide accessible independent quick-release pathways and visible performer stop signals.
- Do not pull an injured performer, apply real circulatory restriction, suspend a performer from decorative cords, simulate traction through actual joints or place live loads across the throat/ribs.
- Test wardrobe, breakaway hardware and all guide movement without performers before rehearsing. Reset to a documented neutral state before every take.

## Ade's first impact

The apparent throw in Sequence 20 is a stunt design, not a real uncontrolled collision. Plan camera separation, edited cause/effect, a padded and dressed contact surface, appropriate stunt doubling where required, controlled body placement and rehearsed actor stop conditions with the qualified stunt team.

The scene establishes a **right shoulder/rib injury**; all later business uses the good arm or compensates visibly. Wardrobe, makeup, actor movement, blocking and sound must maintain that restriction through the final shrine sequence.

## Breath, blood and body-horror performance

- Represent Malik's respiratory distress through acting, editing, wardrobe movement and sound design; never restrict actual breathing or compress the performer's chest.
- Represent Kai's ocular redness, nosebleed, vomiting, tremor and instability using makeup-approved effects, controlled eyelines and separately approved reset procedures. Do not require unsafe ocular products or repeated harmful physical strain.
- Represent Ade's ear pressure, blood and vestibular loss through performance, safe camera grammar and approved makeup. Do not induce genuine dizziness or disorientation in performers.
- Obtain performer consent and scene-specific accessibility accommodations; allow immediate stops without requiring an actor to complete a take.
- Any clinical portrayal or treatment detail requires review by an appropriately qualified professional before production.

## Sound, low-frequency and sensory access

- Unauthorized pressure: irregular, rough-edged low rhythm; familiar correction: slower rounded pulse. Distinguish shape and timing before adding volume.
- Create perceived weight through layered design, silence, practical object vibration and editorial contrast rather than unsafe on-set playback.
- Establish actual playback limits, crew exposure duration, hearing protection, monitoring, accessibility accommodations and distribution presentation with qualified sound/safety personnel. This document sets no unverified numerical thresholds.
- Offer clear advance notice for potentially distressing low-frequency effects, intense sound, flashing or perceptual disruption. Avoid relying on aggressive strobe effects for story comprehension.
- Ensure the two-signature distinction remains readable with accessible captioning, reduced bass playback, ordinary speakers or sound muted through the parallel water/line visual language.

## Color and threshold effects

- Unauthorized signature appears only as restrained bruised violet/blue-black in water, reflection or tarnished metal.
- Familiar corrective response appears only as restrained aged gold/pale bone along the threshold or reflected pattern.
- Red remains a load indicator, never a magical glow or bloodline signal.
- Prefer practical reflection, controlled projection, lighting flags and selective finishing; avoid saturated hero-villain colors, laser beams, portal imagery and competing creature reveals.
- Keep the familiar Entity partial, articulated incorrectly and difficult to resolve. The unauthorized presence never receives a body or separate creature design.

## Shot-planning minimum

1. Establish the stair/door/working-circle/return geography before any severe distortion.
2. Establish the enamel cup or basin clearly before asking it to carry story information.
3. Establish the fixed path from Malik's left wrist to the return stop and river guide.
4. Show Ade's first failed intervention as a blocked route, not a mysterious attack.
5. In the final correction, show the reopened route before implying pressure transfers through Ade.
6. Let Malik's central index movement complete only after Kai identifies the direction and Ade makes the outlet available.
7. Show the door close and lines release before treating the chamber event as survived.
8. Keep shrine restoration a separate location, journey and physical action; do not dissolve directly from chamber success into a magically repaired fitting.

## Sign-off gates

Before rehearsing or filming, require review and explicit sign-off appropriate to the production by the director, production designer, first assistant director, qualified stunt coordinator, qualified rigging professional, costume/props leads, sound department, safety lead, relevant performer representatives and any necessary medical/accessibility advisers.

This plan expresses creative intent and obvious anti-hazard boundaries. It is **not** engineering certification, medical advice, a legal compliance determination or authorization to perform a stunt.
