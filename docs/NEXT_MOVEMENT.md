## Immediate objective

Develop Sequence XI as the **first human and practical consequence** of the two-record discovery.

Do not treat the maintenance room as permission to explain the mythology.

The previous movement earned its power by stopping before interpretation. Sequence XI must begin from what the characters can now *do*, *fear*, *disagree about*, or *refuse* — not from a lecture about what the records mean.

The sequence should answer **one practical question** and create a sharper human one.

## New foundational Solomon lock

Solomon did not leave behind a clean succession plan.

He did not know that his sons would become stewards. He did not secretly appoint Malik and Kai. He could not know whether they possessed the relevant capacities, whether they would understand the work, or whether they would voluntarily accept it.

After his sister died, Solomon's grief and guilt made him increasingly incapable of rebuilding the partnership the work required. He continued maintaining what he could. He returned to Calabar. He preserved procedural records. He kept his sister's perceptual notebooks. He attempted to compensate for her absence.

But he never solved succession.

What he finally left his sons was **access without appointment**:

- the house;
- the maintenance room;
- both bodies of records;
- the material history of the work;
- enough evidence that they could discover the truth and decide what to do.

This came from genuine belief in his children, but also fear.

Solomon could not bring himself to ask them directly, train them openly, expose them deliberately to the danger that killed his sister, or risk hearing them refuse.

His belief in them therefore has a moral contradiction:

> He left them a path, and he abandoned them inside a problem he was too frightened to explain.

Both are true.

Do not make Solomon an omniscient posthumous mastermind. He did not predict Kai's breach. He did not design a supernatural test. He did not know the boys would accept stewardship.

## Sequence XI dramatic target

Sequence XI should no longer be built around the idea that Solomon simply "failed to finish a repair before he died."

That may be materially true in some limited sense, but it is not the dramatic center.

The stronger movement is the brothers discovering evidence that Solomon **prepared access but withheld instruction**.

The records should contain enough order to prove Solomon expected the work to outlive him, while conspicuously lacking anything that appoints a successor or explains the whole system.

That absence is character evidence.

Strong direction:

1. **Stay in daylight.** Let ordinary reality press back in after the supernatural escalation.
2. **Let Malik search for procedure and find a succession gap.** Dates, intervals, measurements, indexed materials, repeated maintenance — then no clean next-person instruction.
3. **Do not turn the gap into a puzzle Solomon designed.** It is not an escape room or posthumous test. The incompleteness comes from grief, fear, avoidance, and a man doing what he could while refusing the part he could not face.
4. **Let the house itself become evidence.** Solomon left ownership/access in a form his sons could reach, but did not bind them to the work through explicit command.
5. **Let Kai understand the emotional shape before the metaphysical one.** Their father left evidence but did not tell them what they were walking into. Kai, whose attachment wound is Solomon's silence, should feel the coldness of that choice.
6. **Let Malik see the other side.** Solomon preserved enough that they are not completely blind. Malik may recognize this as imperfect preparation rather than pure abandonment — without excusing it.
7. **Give Ade the contradiction.** Ade is best positioned to see that both brothers can be right: Solomon trusted them and failed them. He does not need to state that thesis cleanly; his dramatic action should prevent the argument collapsing into saint-versus-coward.
8. **Keep Bode bounded.** He may know Solomon worried about what would happen after him, but unless established by evidence he cannot claim Solomon's private intent with certainty.
9. **Keep Eno practical.** Whatever Solomon intended, there are living people in this house now. Intent does not remove present risk.
10. **End on a choice the brothers make that Solomon refused to make for them.** That is the beginning of voluntary succession architecture without naming succession.

## The practical discovery

The records should reveal a **continuity problem**, not a magical repair recipe.

Useful evidence may include:

- maintenance intervals extending beyond Solomon's last completed visit;
- organized materials/records preserved for future use without a named recipient;
- a recurring procedural cycle whose next date is now approaching or missed;
- a deliberately accessible index or key to Solomon's records;
- no page labeled for Malik, Kai, Ade, "successor," "heir," or chosen replacement;
- the house/room/property arrangements providing access while the work itself remains unassigned.

The point is not "Solomon knew Malik would do X."

The point is:

> Solomon knew someone would eventually have to decide what happened next, and he could not make that decision for his sons — or bring himself to prepare them honestly for it.

## Sequence XI harder question

The old question:

> What, exactly, did Solomon fail to finish before he died?

is now subordinate to the stronger question:

> Did Solomon leave them a path — or abandon them inside a problem he was too afraid to explain?

The long-form answer is **both**, but Sequence XI should not package that answer into a speech.

Let the brothers discover and argue from evidence.

## Character execution law

Use `docs/CHARACTER_VOICE_CONSTITUTION.md` as an active screenplay constraint.

### Malik

- searches for sequence, responsibility, maintenance logic, and what must happen next;
- sees evidence of imperfect preparation where Kai may initially see only abandonment;
- does not suddenly forgive Solomon;
- does not infer that he is Solomon's successor;
- his danger is converting uncertainty into unilateral responsibility.

### Kai

- experiences the missing explanation as a continuation of Solomon's lifelong silence;
- reports concrete absences in the records rather than giving a thesis speech;
- carries guilt for the breach without accepting that this obligates him to inherit anything;
- must remain capable of saying no.

### Ade

- tracks what the brothers' competing interpretations are doing to them;
- recognizes that trust and abandonment can coexist without needing supernatural authority;
- prevents Solomon from becoming either saint or villain;
- must have a consequential action/decision in the sequence.

### Bode

- concrete memory;
- qualified certainty;
- may remember Solomon's fear or avoidance but cannot narrate Solomon's private master plan;
- never fills gaps merely because the plot wants information.

### Eno

- house, family, bodies, time, meals, children, safety, authority;
- whatever Solomon believed, she judges what his silence has done to the living;
- does not debate metaphysics.

The current pressure can come from:

- disagreement over whether access creates obligation;
- the risk to the household;
- Kai's changed relationship to looking;
- Malik's temptation to convert uncertainty into unilateral procedure.

## Final operating rule

Solomon's final act must not become a clever dead-man puzzle.

He preserved access because he could not bear to appoint.

He believed in his children because he loved them.

He left them unprepared because he was afraid.

Sequence XI begins when the sons realize they now have to make the choice their father refused to make for them.
