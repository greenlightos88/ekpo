# EKPO — BRASS OBJECT CONTINUITY

Status: LOCKED / ACTIVE OBJECT AUTHORITY
Effective: 2026-08-17
Authority: explicit creator canon

## Purpose

This document prevents the two major brass objects from collapsing into one continuity object or mutating into a separate magic system.

## Locked distinction

EKPO contains two historically related but physically distinct regulatory brass objects.

### A. Retired sister-era brass carving

Current screenplay appearance:

- discovered by Kai in Solomon's blue drawer in Vancouver in Sequence 1;
- visible by silhouette in the scratched Calabar photograph showing young Solomon, Bode, and their sister;
- later compared by Kai against Oku boundary/regulator design language.

Writer-level history:

- originated in the regulatory architecture during the period when Solomon and his sister performed complementary stewardship together;
- belonged to their shared work rather than to the sister personally;
- was retired from active regulatory use after the catastrophic rupture in which Solomon failed his procedural side and his sister restored equilibrium and died;
- remained in Solomon's custody;
- was eventually carried by Solomon to Canada and hidden among ordinary household remnants.

Emotional function:

The carving is Solomon's material memento of:

- his sister;
- their functioning partnership;
- the regulator as it existed while she was alive;
- the failure he spent the rest of his life trying to compensate for.

This is consistent with Solomon's psychology: he preserves grief as objects, records, maintenance, and custody rather than speaking it plainly.

Prohibitions:

- not the active talisman Kai removes;
- not the sister's personal magical amulet;
- not a soul vessel;
- not a hereditary badge of office;
- not a prison key;
- not a complete repair component by itself.

### B. Active Solomon-era replacement brass talisman

Current screenplay appearance:

- sits inside the hidden regulator in Sequence 6;
- carries related three-line design language with a different center/hollow treatment;
- is deliberately removed by Kai;
- is disclosed to the family in Sequence 9;
- remains under ordinary clean cloth in the Oku house.

Writer-level history:

After the catastrophic rupture and the sister's death, Solomon rebuilt/stabilized the procedural side of the regulator. During that post-catastrophe work, he installed a new active brass talisman to replace the retired sister-era component.

Function:

- active regulatory component;
- part of the maintained physical alignment system;
- removal contributes directly to regulator destabilization.

Prohibitions:

- does not contain the Entity;
- is not the Entity's prison key;
- is not a weapon;
- is not a battery;
- cannot be assumed to repair the breach merely by being put back.

## Why the two objects resemble one another

They belong to the same regulatory tradition/system and therefore share design language. They are not duplicates. Existing screenplay distinctions are intentional evidence of generations of workmanship:

- related three-line motif;
- differing angles;
- differing hollow/center treatment;
- different wear and custody histories.

The relationship should feel like an old component and its later replacement, not two magical collectibles.

## Solomon's memento logic

The retired piece is the last surviving handled object Solomon keeps from the period when his sister was alive and their partnership still functioned.

Its importance is emotional and historical, not a new supernatural ability.

Solomon does not display or explain it. He carries it to Canada, hides it among ordinary household remnants, and preserves it privately. That is consistent with the way he converts grief into custody, systems, objects, and work instead of language.

## Reveal control

The full relationship is **writer-level canon only** at the current screenplay position.

The brothers presently have enough evidence to notice that the objects rhyme, but not enough to know:

- which came first;
- that the Vancouver carving was retired after the sister-era catastrophe;
- that Solomon installed the active replacement afterward;
- why Solomon took the retired piece to Canada;
- that he kept it as a memento of his sister/shared work/failure.

The reveal must be earned materially through one or more of:

- dated records;
- replacement notation;
- measurements;
- wear/fit differences;
- old photographs;
- Solomon's travel/maintenance history;
- a credible physical comparison.

Do not solve it through an unearned Bode exposition speech.

## `BRASS` ledger rule

`BRASS` in Solomon's procedural index is a **material/work category**, not automatically the name of the active talisman. It may include:

- talisman inspection;
- markers;
- anchor points;
- fittings;
- cleaning;
- replacement pieces;
- other brass regulatory hardware already supported by the maintenance room.

Sequence 14 may therefore treat `BRASS` as unresolved until the records identify the specific object or task.

## Dramatic payoff

The page-one carving gains retrospective emotional force without changing its early behavior. Kai unknowingly holds a physical remnant of the partnership Solomon lost before he ever sees the active replacement in the regulator.

That connection deepens Solomon rather than turning him into a posthumous puzzle-master. He kept the old piece because he could not discard what it represented, while still failing to tell his sons the truth.

## Anti-drift laws

- Never merge the two brass objects into one physical object.
- Never call the retired carving the sister's magical amulet.
- Never imply her spirit inhabits it.
- Never make all brass supernatural.
- Never use either object as a one-step repair solution.
- Never make Kai's original discovery proof that Solomon planned Kai's breach.
- Never reveal the complete history before the dramatic evidence earns it.

## Current custody

Retired sister-era carving: part of Solomon's Vancouver effects and the brothers' inherited evidence. Its exact immediate physical location should be re-established the next time it is handled on-page rather than silently teleported between locations.

Active replacement talisman: Oku house, under ordinary clean cloth following Sequence 9; not returned to the regulator.
