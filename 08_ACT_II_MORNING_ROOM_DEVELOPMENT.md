# EKPO ACT II - MORNING CONSEQUENCE AND THE CLOSED ROOM

Version: Sequence X Canonical Development

Mythology authority: `EKPO_Canonical_Mythology_Refactor.md`. The room contains
records of covenant stewardship, not containment.

## Governing purpose

Sequence X converts the first shared supernatural disturbance into a family
decision.

The sequence does not escalate the Entity. It changes the brothers'
understanding of responsibility:

- Malik cannot protect the family simply by leaving
- Kai cannot repair the breach simply by returning the talisman
- Ade cannot keep the brothers together without forcing a shared decision
- Bode cannot preserve restraint through omission after the breach is known
- Eno cannot protect her household while Bode keeps its practical history
  behind a closed door

The red-corded room reveals labour, not lore.

---

# Starting condition

Morning after Sequence IX.

- none of the brothers has slept
- the wrapped talisman remains at the center of the kitchen table
- the empty urn is back in its padded case beside Malik
- Bode has spent the remaining hours reconstructing the broken knot with
  ordinary red cotton
- the original snapped thread remains separate from his failed copies
- Eno has moved the children and uninvolved relatives toward the front of the
  compound
- Eyo is helping her without receiving a supernatural explanation
- the red-corded household door remains closed
- no one has attempted to return to the regulator

---

# Ending condition

- the brothers do not leave the land that morning
- no one returns to the regulator
- Bode deliberately opens the red-corded room
- the room is established as a practical family work room containing records,
  replacement materials, maps, tools, correspondence, and incomplete
  observations
- the brothers learn that the brass object was never the whole restraint
- the brothers learn that Solomon continued participating from Canada
- Eno learns that Bode concealed sustained family labour inside her house
- handwriting in an older record is identified as belonging to Solomon and
  Bode's dead younger sister
- her canonical death, the Entity's nature, and any repair sequence remain
  unexplained to the characters

---

# Supernatural state

Phase Two - Attachment.

No new capability is permitted in Sequence X.

The Entity does not:

- speak
- move an object
- alter a shadow
- interrupt breakfast
- appear in the work room
- punish Bode for opening the door
- use the sister's handwriting
- provide a repair instruction

Daylight consequence is the dramatic pressure. The absence of a new
manifestation keeps the previous night consequential instead of disposable.

The Entity remains a manifestation of inherited imbalance expressed through
disturbed ecology and altered attention, not a creature waiting behind the
next door.

---

# Sequence question

Do the brothers leave the land, or remain long enough to understand what Kai
disturbed?

Answer:

They remain together long enough to inspect the human work Solomon concealed.
This is not consent to inherit it and not agreement to perform a repair.

# Scene Bible

## Movement One - Breakfast under restriction

1. Morning compound life continues in the front rooms and veranda.
2. Eno redirects children and food away from the back passage through ordinary
   household authority.
3. Eyo assists without becoming a frightened extra or exposition source.
4. The brothers remain physically close at the kitchen table and have not
   slept.
5. Breakfast is present but largely untouched.
6. The wrapped talisman occupies the stable center of the table.
7. Bode compares the snapped shrine knot with several failed reconstructions
   made from ordinary red cotton.
8. Malik announces that the brothers should leave the land that morning.
9. His argument is consent and protection: Solomon's request is complete, and
   the family should not absorb consequences hidden from them.
10. Kai refuses to leave the talisman in Eno's house and argues that he must
    accept responsibility for the breach.
11. Bode refuses an immediate return because replacing one object cannot
    reproduce the human work surrounding its placement.
12. Ade refuses both separation and a blame-only argument. He identifies that
    departure may relocate the pressure without understanding it.
13. Eno stops the men from using her household as an abstraction while
    withholding the practical reason for the tied door.
14. Under pressure, Bode reveals one limited fact: the restraint was checked,
    replaced, and recorded across time; Solomon continued participating after
    leaving Nigeria.
15. Malik requires evidence rather than another inherited instruction.
16. Bode chooses to open the red-corded room.

## Movement Two - Opening the household boundary

1. Eno orders Eyo to keep the children in the front part of the compound.
2. Bode approaches the door with the old red cord still intact.
3. He does not cut or break it.
4. He works the knot open by hand, coils the cord, and keeps it.
5. The door sticks through disuse and humidity; opening it is physical, not
   ceremonial.
6. Eno remains at the threshold. She does not defer ownership of her house to
   Bode's secrecy.

## Movement Three - First discoveries

1. Morning light reveals a used work room, not an occult chamber.
2. A scarred table holds ordinary awls, brushes, pliers, measuring cord, wax,
   and brass-cleaning cloths.
3. Shelves hold replacement red cotton wrapped against humidity.
4. Boundary maps have been corrected by hand rather than presented as a
   puzzle.
5. Notebooks record dates, rainfall, river height, damaged stones, and replaced
   material without explaining why the practice works.
6. Envelopes and folded sheets from Vancouver carry Solomon's handwriting.
7. Their range proves that the work continued across decades and distance.
8. Malik recognizes his father's domestic language of lists and corrections.
9. Ade finds the human fracture: Bode's questions remain with Solomon's
   practical replies, while personal matters go unanswered.
10. Kai reaches toward an older notebook, stops himself, and waits for Bode's
    permission.
11. The notebook contains a third handwriting alongside Solomon's and Bode's.
12. Eno recognizes the hand as their younger sister's.
13. The sequence stops before anyone explains what happened to her or how her
    work ended.

# Character matrix

## Malik

Objective:

Remove his brothers from danger and restore a boundary he can understand.

Avoidance:

Admitting that departure may be another form of abandoning responsibility to
the relatives who remain.

Turn:

The work room proves that Solomon's silence organized other people's lives for
decades.

Residue:

Malik's anger becomes more precise. He does not forgive Solomon, but he can no
longer reduce the burden to Kai's theft.

## Ade

Objective:

Keep the brothers from choosing incompatible actions under fear.

Avoidance:

Using humor to create a false consensus.

Turn:

He establishes a temporary decision: nobody leaves and nobody returns alone.

Residue:

Ade becomes the architect of collective action without becoming a mythology
expert.

## Kai

Objective:

Take responsibility without surrendering the right to understand what he
disturbed.

Avoidance:

Treating return as a simple reversal that would erase his second choice.

Turn:

He stops his own hand before touching the sister's notebook and waits for
permission.

Residue:

Kai's curiosity becomes more ethical without becoming passive.

## Bode

Objective:

Prevent an impulsive return while preserving only the uncertainty that is
honest.

Avoidance:

Exposing how much of his adult life was organized around Solomon's instructions
and silence.

Turn:

He opens the room voluntarily.

Residue:

Bode trades control of family knowledge for the possibility of shared
responsibility. His practical competence remains; his moral authority is now
open to judgment.

## Eno

Objective:

Protect the living household and end Bode's use of her home as a container for
unexplained risk.

Avoidance:

None. Her limit is knowledge, not courage.

Turn:

She requires Bode to open the door and recognizes her sister's handwriting
inside.

Residue:

Eno learns that the concealed labour was larger than she knew. She does not
gain supernatural expertise.

## Eyo

Objective:

Keep breakfast, children, and household movement functioning.

Turn:

He accepts Eno's changed arrangement without being reduced to obedience or
fear.

Residue:

The compound remains inhabited by people with practical lives outside the
hidden burden.

  knowledge

- what name is accurate

# Director's notebook

## Camera

- begin in daylight with ordinary food, household movement, and exhausted
  bodies
- keep the wrapped talisman visible without photographing it as an evil object
- treat Bode's failed knots as evidence of fallible memory, not occult
  expertise
- hold the family in shared frames during the argument
- let Bode cross the passage only after a decision has been made
- photograph the door opening through hands, resistance, and ordinary
  architecture
- reveal the work room through wear patterns before readable documents
- do not push into inserts that turn maps or notebooks into puzzle clues
- end on Eno recognizing handwriting, not on a supernatural image

## Sound

- children, dishes, cutlery, and front-veranda conversation continue beyond
  the restricted back passage
- no score under the family argument
- cotton pulls softly against Bode's fingers
- the door opens with swollen wood dragging over tile
- paper, drawer runners, and ordinary tools replace supernatural sound design
- no voice, beat, dog pattern, or ecological silence recurs

## Performance

- Malik's demand to leave is an act of care, not cowardice
- Kai's guilt must not become eagerness
- Ade's mediation should cost effort; he is tired and frightened
- Bode's pauses are acts of selection, not generic mystery
- Eno reads people before objects
- recognition of the sister's handwriting is family memory, not occult
  detection

# Motif progression

## Red cord

Moves from hidden restraint to repeated human labour. Bode opens rather than
breaks the household cord.

## Brass

The covered talisman remains one component among ordinary tools and records. It
is not framed as evil.

## Empty urn

Remains beside Malik after Solomon's ashes are gone. He continues carrying the
case because responsibility survived the body.

## Boundary

The family chooses to open one household boundary deliberately after Kai broke
another alone.

## Handwriting

Turns Solomon's abstract secrecy into sustained human action and restores the
dead sister to the family record without explaining her death.

# Integrity audit

Scene necessity: PASS

The sequence converts supernatural consequence into a family decision and
opens the next investigative movement.

Story progression: PASS

The brothers move from immediate fear to controlled discovery without returning
to the regulator or receiving a repair plan.

Emotional progression: PASS

Malik's protection, Kai's guilt, Ade's mediation, Bode's complicity, and Eno's
household authority collide over one decision.

Character integrity: PASS

No character becomes a genre function or mythology lecturer.

Mythology restraint: PASS

Only repeated maintenance and distributed labour are revealed.

Supernatural constitution: PASS

The Entity does nothing new and is not framed as evil, predatory, sleeping,
imprisoned, or the purpose of the covenant.

Cultural integrity: WORKING PASS

No new cultural authority is invented. Household behavior and material details
require a native Calabar/Efik and production-design review.

Motif continuity: PASS

Red cord, brass, urn, boundaries, handwriting, and family labour change meaning
without multiplying motifs.

Pacing: PASS

One breakfast conflict drives directly to one controlled room reveal.

Could this belong to another film?: NO

The sequence depends on Solomon's urn, Kai's breach, Bode's physical memory,
Eno's authority over the Oku house, the scratched sister, and the family's
cross-Atlantic maintenance history.
